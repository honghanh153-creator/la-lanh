# Tarot: interpreting the card in the question's context

## What changed

The local renderer previously repeated the same meaning regardless of whether the question concerned work or a relationship. Browser QA exposed this with Ace of Pentacles: a conversation question received wording about a work opportunity.

The relationship bank now covers all 78 selected card IDs. Each entry has a contextual meaning, a pitfall, one possible action and a separate hypothetical example. These are original Vietnamese editorial interpretations, not excerpts from books or factual claims about either person.

Source card identity, title, selection order and spread position remain unchanged. Context changes the interpretation, not the drawn cards. The renderer version is `tarot-renderer-v4`; rewrite request fingerprints include the resulting blueprint and provenance.

The four additional banks now contain 312 authored entries: work, communication, energy and self-care, each covering 78 cards. General and relationships remain separately audited. Core meaning, pitfall, possible action and labelled example are distinct fields; the topic banks are not generated by swapping a topic noun into a common sentence.

## Meaning context and Luna

The locally constructed `tarot-meaning-brief-v1` records the selected topic, question intent, recognised closed goal codes, local summary and supported cross-card links. Eight specific goals currently cover apology, arranging a meeting, shared care, handing off work, recognition, workload, rest and disagreement. Unclassified questions remain general; this is not a comprehensive understanding of all questions.

Raw questions, names and birth data are not supplied to Luna. It receives the authored meaning brief and per-position interpretation only after separate external-rewrite consent. A single rewrite is checked before atomic publication. A bad rewrite keeps the local revision; no second paid model is called to rescue it. Missing briefs in legacy queued requests are rejected before provider transmission.

Three editorial cross-card rules are currently supported, each bound to card identity, topic and position. Other spreads receive a position-based summary, not a claim that every possible pair has been synthesised. A one-card draw has no invented pair.

Recognised purposes now constrain the possible action at each position, not just the introduction. The purpose bank has 24 authored action variants (eight goals × three roles). Multiple recognised goals are retained; card meanings and pitfalls still come from the selected card/context bank. Summary warnings carry an explicit caution frame rather than an imperative. A next-step summary keeps the card's contextual meaning separate from the purpose-specific suggestion.

The rewrite gate adds a conservative polarity tripwire on detailed readings, headline, overview and closing question. A new negation overlapping a positive source assertion is rejected unless it preserves an existing caution. This catches the reproduced “không cần lắng nghe” inversion; it does not prove semantic entailment and can reject an otherwise acceptable paraphrase. The local baseline remains available without a second paid call.

## Example

Question: “Mình nên nói rõ chuyện đang khó chịu với một người thế nào?”

Ace of Pentacles in a relationship: “Muốn biến lời hẹn thành thời gian gặp nhau thật.”

Example: “Bạn nhận một lời mời gặp nhưng hai người chưa chọn ngày và chỗ cụ thể.”

Possible action: “Đề nghị một ngày gặp vừa với lịch của cả hai.”

This is a possible way to examine the question, not proof that a meeting was offered. The UI labels it as an example and keeps the disclaimer separate.

## Review gates

- Every relationship card must have an explicit entry; missing entries fail closed.
- Fact gate rejects unknown, substituted or altered card records and inconsistent spread positions.
- Runtime review checks empty sections, opaque copy, repeated boilerplate and excessive scan length.
- The rotating synthetic audit covers 8,424 contextual and 11,232 recognised-purpose spreads, plus deck checks. It is not all possible card combinations or a human comprehension study.
- A regression test verifies that relationship and work interpretations differ while card records remain identical.
- Corpus review and ordinary readers remain necessary: a rule-based gate cannot certify usefulness or prove that a model answered every specific question.

## Scope and remaining acceptance

Previously stored readings remain their saved revisions; no silent migration or paid rewrite was performed. Live Luna fluency/meaning preservation has not been retested with a paid request. Keyword overlap and unsafe-phrase checks are known-regression guards, not a semantic guarantee. A separate reviewed sample corpus and real reader feedback are still needed before promising comprehension.

Ten synthetic question cases are saved in [the local purpose corpus](../reviews/security-content-2026-10-09/tarot-purpose-corpus.md). They are authored QA inputs, not evidence from ten real users.

Local gate rejection on the first draw preserves the chosen cards with `state=complete, reading=null`. The UI explains that the interpretation could not open and offers return/deletion; it does not ask the reader to pick another card or enqueue AI without a valid baseline.

## Research boundary

Joan Bunning's [lesson on card pairs](https://www.learntarot.com/less15.htm) provides a primary reference for comparing contrasting and reinforcing meanings. That supports the next synthesis layer; it does not validate predictions or establish this matrix as scientifically predictive. No claim is made that entire copyrighted books were ingested or that an AI output was a professional consultation.
